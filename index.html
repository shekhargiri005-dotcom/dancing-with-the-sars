The Langtang Collapse

EVENT: ICE AVALANCHE

LOCATION: LANGTANG VALLEY, NEPAL (28.25°N, 85.50°E)

SENSOR: NISAR L-BAND (24.2 CM)

01 // What Happened

A massive ice-rock avalanche detached from the steep hanging glaciers of Langtang Lirung peak, plummeting thousands of meters down narrow mountain chutes. Over 20 million cubic meters of ice, pulverized granite bedrock, and degrading permafrost sheared away along structurally weakened joint planes, generating a devastating atmospheric shockwave that echoed across the valley before settling into a deep deposit field.

02 // The Damage

The tumbling wall of debris pulverized downstream pastoral settlements, obliterating bridges, trails, and high-altitude weather stations. The debris choked the valley gorge, creating an unstable natural landslide dam that impounded glacial meltwater—threatening downstream Himalayan villages with a secondary catastrophic dam-breach flash flood.

03 // How Radar Saw It

While thick monsoon cloud sheets and dense mountain fog completely blinded optical satellites, NISAR's 24.2 cm L-band radar penetrated the storm cover. Differential amplitude tracking isolated a massive +6.2 dB backscatter surge across the chaotic debris deposit, while interferometric phase coherence revealed 11.4 cm of precursor slope creep prior to the detachment.

01 // What Happened

High above Juneau, Alaska, Suicide Basin—a marginal glacial basin dammed by the towering flank of the Mendenhall Glacier—accumulated tens of millions of cubic meters of subglacial meltwater. Once hydrostatic water pressure overcame the ice dam's structural seal, subglacial drainage conduits ruptured violently, initiating an uncontainable jökulhlaup lake outburst.

02 // The Damage

More than 31 million cubic meters of glacial discharge surged downstream into Mendenhall Lake and River, setting record-shattering peak flood stages. Extreme bank erosion caused residential homes to collapse into the river, tore out arterial roadway networks, and caused extensive municipal damage across Juneau while cloud cover concealed the basin from ground and aerial view.

03 // How Radar Saw It

Heavy Pacific coastal storm fronts and continuous mountain fog kept optical cameras blind, but NISAR's L-band radar pierced directly through the weather. Because calm lake water reflects radar pulses specularly away from the satellite (registering as a pitch-black radar signature below -24 dB), NISAR mapped the sudden loss of 168 hectares of water surface, accurately quantifying flood discharge volumes in real time.

    01 // TIMELINE OF EVENTS

    06:45 IST • PRE-COLLAPSE

    Precursory Slope Destabilization

    Deep permafrost degradation in bedrock joints and sub-surface fracture dilation along the 5,600m crown ridge of Ronti peak trigger catastrophic brittle shear stress.

    10:21 IST • DETACHMENT

    Massive Bedrock & Hanging Glacier Failure

    A ~27 million m³ triangular wedge composed of 80% quartz-mica gneiss and 20% glacial ice detaches instantaneously, free-falling 1,800m down vertical chutes onto the Ronti glacier floor.

    10:25 IST • TRANSFORMATION

    Hyper-Concentrated Debris Slurry Mobilization

    Frictional heat generated by kinetic impact pulverizes and melts the glacier ice, converting dry rock mass into a hyper-concentrated slurry travelling at over 60 m/s (216 km/h) down Ronti Gad valley.

    10:32 IST • FIRST IMPACT

    Destruction of Rishiganga Hydroelectric Plant

    The 20-meter mud-rock wall slams into the 13.2 MW Rishiganga power project, demolishing concrete headworks and sweeping away the arterial valley bridge in seconds.

    10:45 IST • SECOND IMPACT

    Inundation of NTPC Tapovan Vishnugad Tunnels

    The floodwave surges into the 520 MW NTPC Tapovan Vishnugad dam site, burying the intake barrage and choking underground headrace tunnels with tens of thousands of tons of sediment.

    02 // HUMAN IMPACT

    The disaster resulted in over 200 fatalities and missing persons, predominantly engineers and labor crews trapped inside the subterranean tunnel network of the NTPC Tapovan Vishnugad hydroelectric facility. Both the 13.2 MW Rishiganga project and the 520 MW Tapovan Vishnugad barrage suffered total or near-total destruction, inflicting an estimated $180 million in direct infrastructural damages. Over a dozen high-altitude Himalayan villages across the Chamoli district were completely isolated following the destruction of bridges and roads, prompting a massive joint military and civil disaster response under harsh alpine conditions.

    03 // RADAR PHYSICS ANALYSIS

    Retrospective InSAR (Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar) processing revealed progressive, accelerated slope creep of up to 12.8 cm over the 3.5 years leading up to the 2021 failure along the crown fracture of Ronti peak. Following the collapse, multi-temporal radar backscatter (γ°) showed a dramatic surge of up to +8.4 dB across the valley deposition zone due to rough surface volume scattering from fractured blocky rubble. Simultaneously, interferometric coherence dropped precipitously across the 30 km flood track, isolating the scoured path where high-velocity debris stripped soil and riparian vegetation down to polished bedrock.

    GLACIOLOGY FOUNDATION

    How Ice Becomes a Flood: 3 Natural Pathways

    01

    Ice-Rock Avalanche Dam

    A mass collapse of hanging glacier and bedrock fills a canyon, impounding a temporary reservoir that later over-tops or bursts (e.g. Langtang, Chamoli).

    02

    Subglacial Lake Outburst (Jökulhlaup)

    A glacier dam impounds seasonal meltwater. When water pressure overcomes the ice dam weight, the lake drains catastrophically beneath the glacier (e.g. Suicide Basin / Mendenhall).

    03

    Moraine Dam Failure (GLOF)

    A retreating glacier leaves an unstable moraine holding back a deep proglacial lake. Landslides, waves, or ice melt breach the moraine wall (e.g. South Lhonak).
