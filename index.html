01 //

What Happened

A massive 27-million-cubic-meter wedge of rock and hanging glacier detached from the north face of Ronti Peak in the Chamoli district, plummeting 1,800 meters into the valley floor. The catastrophic impact pulverized ice and bedrock into a hyper-turbulent debris flow that surged down the Rishiganga and Dhauliganga valleys, destroying two hydroelectric projects and killing over 200 workers.

02 //

Why It Happened

Decades of climate warming degraded high-altitude permafrost that had bound the heavily fractured rock mass for centuries. Structural weakness along steeply dipping joint planes, combined with warm winter temperatures and freeze-thaw wedge expansion, caused deep tensile fractures to propagate undetected until total shear failure occurred.

03 //

How Radar Sees It

NISAR’s 24.2 cm L-band radar penetrates deep cloud cover, fog, and nighttime darkness to image slope changes. Pre-failure slope creep creates distinctive multi-cycle InSAR fringe anomalies (surface displacement of mm to cm), while post-event radar backscatter registers a massive jump in diffuse surface roughness (+6 dB) across the scarp and depositional dam zone.

04 //

Satellite Data Limitations

Steep Himalayan gorges create radar geometric layover and radar shadow along steep facing slopes, requiring cross-track ascending and descending pass synthesis. Furthermore, the 12-day orbital repeat cycle means real-time flash flood onset cannot be caught mid-descent; NISAR's mission value lies in systematic pre-failure deformation mapping and post-disaster dam stability tracking.

How Ice Becomes a Flood: 3 Natural Pathways

01

Ice-Rock Avalanche Dam

A mass collapse of hanging glacier and bedrock fills a canyon, impounding a temporary reservoir that later over-tops or bursts (e.g. Langtang, Chamoli).

02

Subglacial Lake Outburst (Jökulhlaup)

A glacier dam impounds seasonal meltwater. When water pressure overcomes the ice dam weight, the lake drains catastrophically beneath the glacier (e.g. Suicide Basin / Mendenhall).

03

Moraine Dam Failure (GLOF)

A retreating glacier leaves an unstable moraine holding back a deep proglacial lake. Landslides, waves, or ice melt breach the moraine wall (e.g. South Lhonak).
